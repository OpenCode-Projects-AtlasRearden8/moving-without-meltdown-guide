# Mover quotes, decoded

Moving quotes look like airline pricing had a litter. Here's the plain
version of how movers price, what a real quote includes, the red flags
that say "keep shopping," and the tipping etiquette that trips everyone.

> The one rule worth tattooing on the moving folder: **a real quote is
> written, itemized, and bound to a date.** "We'll figure it out at the
> truck" is not a quote — it's a hostage negotiation with a couch.

## Two ways movers price (both are fine, know which you're getting)

**1. By weight and distance (long distance).** A "binding estimate"
names a final price based on the weight they inventory and the
distance. A "non-binding estimate" is an educated guess that can move
up or down by actual weight — bound by a "105% rule" under federal law
(they can't charge more than 10% over the non-binding estimate at
delivery, though the excess can be billed later). So always ask:
"binding or non-binding?" and write the answer down.

**2. By crew size and hours (local).** Price = hourly rate × crew
size × hours, with a minimum (often 2–3 hours) plus a travel/transfer
fee. This is why decluttering is money: fewer boxes = fewer hours, and
hour 7 costs the same per hour as hour 1.

**Hybrids exist too** (you load a container they haul, or you pack and
they drive). All of those are legitimate arrangements — the only scam
is whoever says "don't worry about the inventory list."

## What to compare (not the total, at first)

| Compare this        | Why it matters                                                              |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Binding or not      | Determines whether the number can move at the door                          |
| Crew size           | Two people move your 3-bedroom slower than three; the per-hour math flips   |
| Minimum hours       | A "cheap $120/hr" quote with a 5-hour minimum is a $600 floor, not $120     |
| Included in hours   | Materials, stair carries, packing help — itemized, or it's an upcharge      |
| Travel fee          | Fuel/driver time from depot; some quote it in, some add at the end          |
| Coverage            | Basic "released value" covers pennies per pound; see [glossary](10-glossary.html) |
| The bold line       | Pack-and-load-vs-load-only — the price gap is your Saturday, quantified      |

Get **two or three written quotes**, compare line by line, and pick
the crew you'd be happy to let into your grandma's house. A $300
spread between quotes is normal; a "80% lower than everyone" quote
means keep shopping.

## Red flags: close the tab, politely

- **No site visit and no virtual survey** for a big move. A real mover
  inventories what they're carrying *before* bidding on it.
- **Large deposits required.** Legitimate movers rarely want big
  prepayment; pay most of it when they deliver. Anything over ~25%
  deposit, walk politely.
- **Company changes its name and phone number between quote and
  loading.** Check: the USDOT / MC number on the truck, paperwork, and
  the federal mover database should all agree.
- **"Binding" estimate that magically reopens at the door** because a
  box "wasn't agreed on." If the inventory list says it's there, it's
  included. Ask for the list.
- **No written paperwork at all**: no inventory, no bill of lading, no
  numbers. The paper is your insurance if anything goes sideways.
- **Fake urgency** — "we can do it TOMORROW if you pay
  tonight!" Good movers have calendars; panic pricing has them too,
  and either way you get to say no. Choose the calendar.

## Weighing in on the day

- Walk the crew in, point out what goes and what stays (a signed
  inventory list, keep your copy).
- Photograph items before loading, especially the TV and anything
  glass-fronted.
- Be the person who says where things go at the new place. Crews are
  excellent at carrying and terrible at psychic floor plans.
- Before signing delivery paperwork, do a quick check: count boxes,
  look for damage, and note it on the form **before** signing. Notes
  on the bill of lading are how claims get taken seriously.

## Tipping: what's normal, minus the panic

- **Local crew:** $20–$40 per person for a normal day; more if they
  moved a piano, less if it rained on everyone equally.
- **Long-distance crew:** often more, at your discretion; the driver
  and the crew are two teams.
- **Not expected, but always noticed:** coffee, water, Gatorade, and
  "lunch is on me" cost less than tips and get remembered kindly.
- Never feel guilt-pressured: tipping is thanks, not a toll.
  (See also: [cost worksheet](09-cost-worksheet.html) has a line for it.)

## The one-sentence summary

Written, itemized, insured, inventory-listed. Hourly quotes love few
hours, weight quotes love light households, and your
[decluttering habit](07-packing-by-zone.html) loves your wallet right back.
