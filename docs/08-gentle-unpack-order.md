# The gentle unpack order

Nobody should unpack a whole house in one weekend. That's the recipe
for the Day-3 crash and the six "stuff we didn't finish" boxes that
stay sealed until the next move. This is an unpack order that respects
that you have a job.

> The philosophy in one line: **unpack in order of how much each thing
> reduces daily friction**, and stop when the evening's one zone is
> done. The rest of the house can be stacks for a week; that's not
> failure, that's a house mid-sentence.

## The rhythm: one zone per evening

| Day      | Evening's one zone                                        | Approx. time |
|----------|-----------------------------------------------------------|--------------|
| Day 1    | Beds, bedding, bathroom basics, chargers (much of this is the [first-night box](06-first-night-box.html)) | 30–45 min |
| Day 2    | Clothes — enough for the work week, not the archive       | 45 min |
| Day 3    | Kitchen: coffee, daily dishes, one full cabinet, trash    | 45 min |
| Day 4    | Kids' and pets' essentials (their sleep is the house's sleep) | 30 min |
| Day 5    | Work setup: laptop, chair, one working desk surface       | 30 min |
| Weekend A| Boxes belong to rooms, not stacks: deliver every box to its room | whatever the crew's wheels allow |
| Week +1  | Kitchen to working-fully, hobbies, decor one wall per evening | evenings |
| Weekend B| Coat closet, linen closet, the "someday" shelf, donate run | one trip |

## The friction test for what comes next

Unsure what to unpack next? Ask: **"What will annoy me most tomorrow
morning if it's still in a box?"** That box wins. Meltdowns are
mostly repeated small annoyances; this test spends them down in order.

## Three permissions

1. **You may leave decor until week +2.** Walls keep.
2. **You may have a "papers" box** that sits until the weekend (one
   box, one label, not forbidden, not infinite).
3. **You may celebrate small.** A working coffee cabinet *is* a
   milestone. Say so out loud. (Nobody hears; that's fine; say it.)

## The two things not to skip

- **Trash and recycle overflow planning.** Unpacking generates a
  cardboard mountain. Flatten as you go; the pile is honest about its
  size when it's flat against a wall.
- **The walk-through habit of the new place.** One photo set now
  (same as the rental method in [walk-through photos](05-rental-walk-through-photos.html)),
  because the move-in condition of *this* place is the deposit story
  of the next move.

## One week later

By the second weekend, most homes can be "functional with visible
edges" — which is the honest end state. The last boxes are allowed to
take their time; the [glossary](10-glossary.html) will still be here,
and so will your back.
