# Utility transfer scripts

Nobody enjoys utility phone calls, so here they are pre-written. Read
the script, skip the maze of options you already know, note what they
say, and move on with your day. Each call takes about five minutes.

**Before any call, have ready:** old address, new address, move-in and
move-out dates, account number (it's on the bill), and a photo of both
places' meters taken with your phone.

**The overlap trick, one sentence:** end services at the old place the
day *after* the trucks leave, and start them at the new place the day
*before* you arrive — a one- or two-day overlap costs about the same as
a pizza and buys you light, water, and the ability to test the stove.

## Electricity and gas

> **You:** "Hi, I'd like to schedule a stop and a start please — I'm
> moving. Stop at [old address] on [date], start service at [new
> address] on [date]."
>
> **Then ask:** "Is start service the same account, or a new account?
> Is there a connection or deposit fee? Does the new address require
> an appointment, or does it turn on remotely?"

**Notes:**
- Some utilities need a deposit or a service call appointment at the
  new address — book it now, not on move day.
- Same-name new account setup is painless if you call during business
  hours; evening calls sometimes route to the after-hours support
  team's cousin (see [glossary](10-glossary.html): "escalation").

## Water and sewer

> **You:** "I'm moving. Please stop water service at [old address] on
> [date] and start it at [new address] on [date]."
>
> **Then ask:** "Is water billed by the city or a private provider? Any
> setup fee or final-read deposit? Do you need a final meter read?"

**Notes:**
- Water is often billed by the **city**, sometimes separately from
  electricity. Ask who bills it — you can call the wrong company for a
  very funny zero minutes.
- Final bills love following you months later. Keep the folder.

## Internet and TV

> **You:** "I'm moving from [old address] to [new address] on [date].
> Can I transfer my plan, or do I need a new order? What's available at
> the new address, and when's the earliest install?"

**Then ask:** "Is there a transfer fee? Equipment return — do I need a
box or drop-off? Can I keep my email or phone number?"

**Notes:**
- Installer slots book out **weeks ahead**. Make this call early (week 6).
- Return the router — providers will bill you monthly until the
  equipment is tracked down. Put the return box and receipt in your
  moving folder; internet at the new place can start week +1 and nobody
  needs that extra invoice surprise.

## Trash, recycling, and city services

> **You:** "I'm moving out of the service area on [date] and into [new
> address] on [date]. Please stop billing at the old address and set up
> service at the new one — can you tell me my pickup days there?"

**Notes:**
- New city = new rules (bin size, recycling day). One call, one answer.
- Ask about **bulk pickup** at the old address — useful the week before
  the truck, because stuff you can't move needs an exit plan of its own.

## Meters: the thirty-second photos

On the way out: photograph **all meters** (electric, gas, water) plus
the readings. On the way in: do it again. That's it — your last read
matches what they bill, and your first read matches what you pay for.
These photos pay for themselves the first time a final bill disagrees.

## After every call, write down

- The date and the company name
- Confirmation number (if given) — or "no confirmation given"
- The agent's first name and the fee or deposit they mentioned

That log lives in your moving folder next to the [master list](02-address-change-master-list.html),
and it settles any bill that shows up confused about its own life.
