# Rental walk-through photos

Two walk-throughs, two photo sets, one deposit. This page is the
photo routine — which rooms to shoot, in what order, and the
before/after trick that makes disputes boring.

> The whole strategy in one sentence: **on move-in day, your photos
> say "it was already like this"; on move-out day, they say "it is
> exactly like that again."**

## Move-in day: the "already like this" set

Shoot these the day you get keys, *before* your furniture arrives —
even mid-paint-disappointment state:

- [ ] **Every room, four wall shots** (or one wide panorama per corner) + floor and ceiling
- [ ] **Damage close-ups**: chipped paint, carpet stains, cracked tiles, dents in doors — right away, with something like a quarter next to it for scale
- [ ] **Appliances**: interior of fridge and oven, washing machine drum, water heater area
- [ ] **Smoke and CO detectors** (proof they exist and look non-ancient)
- [ ] **Meters and any exterior damage** (fence, porch, parking spot)
- [ ] **Existing musty or mildew spots** in bathrooms and under sinks (photo first, never scrub blind)

Then **email the set to yourself and your landlord** the same week,
with a line like: "Photos from move-in, for the file." Dated emails are
the strongest friend a deposit has.

## Move-out day: the mirror set

Aim for the same shots of the same angles. Cleaned-and-empty rooms,
four walls per room, all appliances, all damage spots you can now show
as either fixed or pre-existing. Bonus items:

- [ ] All bulbs working, all fixtures present (photos can prove the set exists)
- [ ] Keys photographed on the counter right before hand-off
- [ ] Final meter photos (same as the [utility scripts](03-utility-transfer-scripts.html) page)
- [ ] A short video walking each room without commentary — videos settle "which wall" arguments

## Small rental damage, calmly handled

A wall anchor is not a catastrophe. Two screws' worth of spackle, a
sanding pass, and five minutes repaints nothing else but your
confidence. Take the "before repair" photo, fix, then photograph the
after. Some property damage is cheaper to fix yourself than to argue
about — that's not defeat, that's budgeting.

## What photos can and can't do

Photos support your written timeline; they don't replace your lease's
move-out condition clause or a signed statement, which are also in
your folder. Keep both — paper wins when it disagrees with memory,
which is why we keep so much of it.
