# Glossary (moving-industry jargon, translated)

Moving companies, leases, and utility companies have their own
vocabulary, which would be charming if it weren't also how bills get
weird. Here's the plain-English version of the words that show up.

**Accessorial** — extra service beyond pick-up and drop-off: stairs,
long carry, piano, appliance handoff, elevator reservations. Ask "does
my quote include accessorials?" and watch the quote grow honest.

**Agent/carrier** — on paper, the company that actually owns the
truck/does the carrying. If two names appear (a broker and an agent),
note both names and both numbers: complaints and claims go to whoever
holds the truck.

**Bill of lading (BOL)** — the contract-and-receipt of your move:
names, addresses, inventory, price, and dates. It's the most important
piece of paper in the truck. Keep yours; it's your claim's ticket.

**Binding estimate** — a quoted final price that can't be raised above
what's written, for the items on the inventory. If items are added
later, the price can change — the point is *you know what you're
buying up front*.

**Broker** — company that arranges your move and hands it to a
carrier. Legal and common, but make sure the carrier is licensed,
federal-registered (USDOT/MC number), and insured — the broker isn't
the one on your driveway.

**Carry distance / long carry** — path from door to truck. Long
carries, stairs, and "no truck access" are honest upcharges. Visit
your own driveway before befriending the quote.

**Crew size** — number of movers on site. Hourly math: more crew
= fewer hours = usually fewer dollars. Two-movers-slow is real.

**Escalation** — any call where the person says "let me transfer you"
and you mysteriously end up in a queue with hold music. Not rude, just
a process. Write down who you last spoke to so you never have to
re-explain your entire biography twice in one evening.

**Full-value protection (FVP)** — mover-provided coverage closer to
"actual item value." Costs more than default coverage, worth it for
anything with a soul or a serial number.

**Inventory list** — writeup of what's moving, room by box. Signed by
you and the crew; it's how disputes end ("it's on the list; where is
it?") instead of becoming philosophy.

**LTL (Less Than Truckload)** — shipping a load that shares a truck
with other people's loads. Cheaper, slower, and your stuff rides with
strangers: fine for boxes, careful for the heirloom.

**Non-binding estimate** — a best guess by weight/distance that can
adjust with actuals; federal rules cap how much over the estimate can
be collected at delivery (see the [quotes page](04-mover-quotes-decoded.html)).
Ask to see the weight ticket if it climbs.

**Order for service** — the go-ahead document naming mover, dates,
services, and price. You get one before loading; the truck doesn't
move on vibes.

**Released value protection** — the free, minimal-by-law coverage
(default ≈ 60¢ per pound per article). A 40 lb. box of books is "worth"
$24 on paper. Great fidelity: $0 extra cost, quietly terrible math.

**Self-storage** — you rent a unit, you carry your own stuff. Month
to month usually; ask about minimum stay, lock fees, and climate
control for anything that melts or rusts.

**Tariff** — the mover's official rate book. If a charge confuses you,
ask "which tariff line?" Honest movers love the tariff; it's their
rules too.

**Transfer/storage-in-transit (SIT)** — your things parked in the
mover's warehouse for days/weeks mid-move. Ask the per-day rate and
who pays if the delay's cause is unclear. This is the one place a
"storage" word can quietly bill you well.

**USDOT / MC number** — license numbers proving a mover is registered
for interstate moves. Check the paperwork and the truck door; all
three should show one matching number (or match the carrier
properly).

**Walk-through survey** — the pre-move visit (in person or video)
where a mover looks at what you actually own. Companies that skip it
for a whole-house move are quoting a fantasy house, possibly yours,
improbably.

*Definitions here are practical explanations, not legal advice; your
quote, lease, and bill of lading define your actual deal.*
